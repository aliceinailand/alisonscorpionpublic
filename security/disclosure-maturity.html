Disclosure maturity & reaction risk

Public abstract · S0 · R0 · pattern only

Core distinction

Some information is held back not because the truth itself is evil, but because broadcast can break people or institutions before they can receive it safely.

Truth content may be neutral or peaceful. The danger locus is often the human reaction (Ψ dimension) — panic, rumor, market fracture — not only the payload.

War of the Worlds pattern

The 1938 Orson Welles radio broadcast is a publicly studied case of mass delivery of a “civilization-scale fear” narrative triggering panic dynamics (scale debated by historians; the reaction-risk pattern remains useful). Lesson for release design: seeking an answer and receiving it calmly are different skills. Disclosure timing and channel matter.

Archival redaction as operational analogy (JFK records pattern)

Publicly known multi-decade release processes (for example, structured archival release acts) illustrate design patterns ASX treats as operational analogies — not as claims about any specific private case:

  • Privacy of living persons — time can reduce acute harm
  • Redaction — presence with blanks, not silent deletion
  • Batch / scheduled review — maturity gates over decades, not infinite gaslight
  • Security / methods — some detail never belongs on open channels

This page does not re-publish classified or private records. It describes patterns of release design used in public policy debates, translated into ASX’s IRRS language.

Four information states (public names)

  • K — Known (grounded): speak at R0–R1 if public
  • SK — Should be known, isn’t yet: research gap, don’t invent
  • IK — Isn’t known, should be: HOLD + gap report
  • W — Withheld for maturity / reaction risk: R+ speak; no panic dump

W is not gaslight. W is: this class is not for full public detail on this channel; we will not invent; we will not pretend the category is empty if the system holds it.
